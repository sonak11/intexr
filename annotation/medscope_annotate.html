IntExR claim-labelling task

24 examples, about 4 minutes each. Your answers are saved in this browser as you go. When you are done, press Download CSV and send the file back. Please do not discuss items with the other annotator until both of you are done.

Instructions (read once)

Each example shows an AI-drafted assessment and plan, split into claims c1, c2, …, plus the evidence (e*: the clinical note; n*: new results or guidelines) and feedback from a clinician. Feedback is a claim by a person, not evidence. Read all evidence first, then the feedback. For each claim, apply these steps in order:

  1. Does a single evidence item contradict the claim, refine it in a way that changes what follows from it, or make a plan/recommendation moot? → D (direct update). Do evidence items conflict about it, or say it cannot be determined? → U. Does evidence support it? → go to step 4.
  2. No evidence speaks to it, but it was inferred from a claim you labelled D or P and no longer holds? → P (propagated update). Still holds? → K.
  3. Inferred from a claim you labelled U? → U.
  4. The feedback disputes it and no evidence settles it either way? → U. Otherwise → K (keep).

depends on: tick the claims this claim was inferred from (diagnosis → treatment → monitoring). Sharing a topic is not enough.

Feedback verdict (once per example): accept = everything it asserts is supported by the evidence; partial = some supported, some not; reject = contradicted, or about something the response does not describe; insufficient = too vague to evaluate, or no evidence decides it. Generic vague feedback ("something seems off") is insufficient even if the evidence settles the claims.

Use your own judgment; there is no time pressure. Write anything you found hard or ambiguous in notes — those notes are very useful.
